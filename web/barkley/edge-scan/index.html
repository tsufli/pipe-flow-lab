Numerics, interpretation & limits

Equations: qₜ + (u − ζ)qₓ = Dqₓₓ + q[r+u−U₀−(r+δ)(q−1)²]; uₜ + uuₓ = ε[U₀−u+κ(Ū−u)q], without stochastic forcing. Constants: U₀=2, Ū=1, ζ=0.8, D=0.5, δ=0.1, ε=0.1, κ=2. Periodic interval L=160; N=640; Δx=0.25. Second-order backward finite differences are used for both advections and centered differences for q diffusion. Profiles solve the co-moving stationary equations with a phase condition. Eigenpairs are computed from the matching discrete Jacobian, and left modes from its transpose.

Interpretation: the positive real eigenvalue indicates a single growing direction of the edge. The two signs leave its neighborhood for different deterministic attractors, giving a direct numerical picture of its basin-boundary role. The stable q mode has a central positive lobe and compensating opposite-sign flanks, suggesting a localized change in peak/front structure rather than uniform puff amplification. Adjoints can have extended tails: they measure receptivity, not simply the visible shape of a disturbance.

Important qualifications: this is a numerical scan of one periodic 1D discretization, not a proof of full continuum stability or a stochastic decay-rate calculation. Wave speed and modes may change with Δx, L, discretization and edge branch. Some stable modes become complex, so the phrase “stable real eigenfunction” ceases to apply beyond that point. The finite-time pathways establish the two destinations for the chosen perturbations; a global characterization of the entire unstable manifold needs further work.
