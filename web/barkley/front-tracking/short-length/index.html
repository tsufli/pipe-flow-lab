Why the edge changes the definition of length

Our saved deterministic decay edge at r ≈ 0.5957 has a low peak, unlike the stable puff. The low-threshold crossing can measure its width while q = 0.5 cannot. The edge is a saddle in full field space, not a special fixed value of the scalar length alone.

Three different reference lengths

The leading ε → 0 theoretical slow-interior length is not the same observable as the width between threshold crossings. Nor is the decay edge a solution of the scalar drift equation Aℓ(ℓ)=0.

Important: The edge profile is from the nearest stored numerical traveling-wave continuation at r ≈ 0.5957, not a freshly converged edge at exactly r = 0.6. Small differences in r, grid and threshold must be kept in mind.

Parameters and conditions

Limitations: Conditional estimates still omit intervals after threshold loss, even before a physical decay is confirmed. This may bias the shortest bins. The low threshold makes weak puffs measurable, but adds sensitivity to spatial noise and multiple components. Branch restarts are a deliberately selected population and cannot be pooled as independent natural runs. These are finite-lag coefficients, not proof of a closed Markovian length diffusion. We have not performed numerical convergence tests.
